## Why
#46: a transfer Stripe refused, such as one blocked by a low platform balance, stayed refused for about 24 hours, because every retry reused `payout:<task_id>` and Stripe replays a saved error for the same key. The worker was told "it will be retried" and was not paid even after the balance was topped up.
#52: `retryPayouts` always took the oldest 25 tasks, so 25 tasks on accounts that never finish onboarding blocked every newer payout. A worker who had texted STOP was also told on the capture page that a setup link had been texted, but no text is sent to an opted-out worker.

## What
- `payoutWorker` lists transfers with `transfer_group=task_<id>` (after the capability check) and returns an existing transfer instead of creating a second one.
- Each payout attempt uses a fresh key, `payout:<task_id>:<db clock µs>`, issued by `eyes_payout_begin`. That function locks the task row, requires `verified`, and stores the key in the stripe_ref of the task's `release` row as a lease. The lease is held until `eyes_payout_end` clears it, or for at most 15 minutes if the process dies. A caller that finds the lease taken skips the task. Retry ticks, onboarding webhooks, captures and operator releases on any number of instances therefore never overlap on one task.
- `eyes_payouts_due(account, limit, after)` orders by `(created_at, id)` from a cursor. `retryPayouts` advances the cursor after every full batch and wraps after a short one, so every due task is tried within ceil(N/25) runs.
- Capture page: an opted-out worker with no payout account gets "Verified. To get your $X, set up payouts here: <connect link>".
- `009_payout_retry.sql`: functions only, EXECUTE revoked from public, anon and authenticated, and the 2-argument `eyes_payouts_due` dropped.

## Decisions & alternatives rejected
See the ADR-012 addendum (T4.9).
- The lease rather than a row lock, because on InsForge every RPC is its own transaction.
- Database clock rather than `p_now`. An instance with a clock 31 minutes ahead (the verifier race file's `late`) treated a live lease as expired and paid twice; the database clock fixed it.
- Release on every outcome rather than holding the lease after unknown outcomes. Holding would delay the onboarding webhook up to 15 minutes after a capability failure. The next attempt's lookup reconciles any transfer that did go through.
- Keyset cursor rather than a backoff window. No column records a failed attempt, and a per-instance skip list starves again once more tasks are stuck than fit in the window.

## Assumptions
- Stripe saves a POST's result, including 400 and 500 errors, once execution starts, replays it for at least 24 hours, and saves nothing for validation failures, concurrent same-key conflicts or rate limits (idempotent_requests and error-low-level docs, fetched 2026-10-17).
- `GET /v1/transfers?transfer_group=` is read-after-write consistent. Stripe's search docs tell callers to use list APIs, which "aren't subject to the availability delays", for read-after-write flows. Results come newest first. `TransferListParams.transfer_group` and `limit` are in stripe 23.0.0 `esm/resources/Transfers.d.ts`.
- stripe-node 23 retries network failures, 409s and 5xx twice by default (`PlatformFunctions.getDefaultMaxNetworkRetries() = 2`), with an 80 s timeout per try, and resends the same headers, so an attempt's network retries reuse its key. Worst case for one attempt (GET account, GET list, POST transfer) is about 12.2 minutes, which is under the 15-minute lease.
- InsForge RPC returns `setof ledger` rows as objects, the same as other table-returning functions.

## Verification
- Payments unit tests (Stripe HTTP stubbed):
  - The payout sends GET account, then GET `/v1/transfers?transfer_group=task_tsk_5&limit=1`, then the keyed POST.
  - An existing transfer in the group is returned with no POST.
  - With origin/main's adapter, both tests fail (2 failed).
- Engine adversarial int tests (Postgres, fakes):
  - Refusal: a Stripe-like fake saves every result by key and refuses the first execution with `balance_insufficient`. The capture is left `verified`, and the next `retryPayouts` pays under a second, different key: 1 transfer, 1 payout row, 1 paid text.
  - Race: two service instances × 10 reps. `retryPayouts`, `markWorkerOnboarded` (both instances) and, on odd reps, a disputed task's operator release run at once. Each task gets exactly 1 transfer, 1 payout row and 1 paid text.
  - Lease: a lease held for 14 min 50 s blocks the onboarding webhook. At 15 min the webhook pays, once.
  - Starvation: 30 tasks on an account that never activates do not block a newer payable task. It is paid within the retry loop, the 30 stay `verified`, and all 30 are paid once the account activates.
  - The capture page for a worker who texted STOP shows the connect link, and that link mints the onboarding URL.
- Old code fails the new tests. With origin/main's engine and no 009, 5 fail: refusal, lease, starvation, the STOP page and the key format in resolve.
- Mutants killed (each on a fresh DB):
  - Lease always granted: race and lease tests fail.
  - Lease never expires: lease test fails.
  - No rotation cursor: starvation test fails.
  - Lease never released: 5 fail.
- The adversarial suite passed 3 times in a row with no flakes (38/38).
- The verifier race file (scratchpad `zz-races.int.test.ts`, S1-S7) was rerun on the new code. Its one-key-per-task assertion was replaced by a `payout:<id>:<n>` format check; the at-most-one-transfer and payout-row conservation checks are unchanged. ## Known gaps
- The connect link on the capture page is plain text. The page renders `message` with `textContent` (apps/web/worker, not owned here), so the worker copies the link rather than tapping it.
- Double pay is still possible if Stripe executes a transfer POST after stripe-node gave up on it (3 × 80 s), or creates one during incident recovery after a 500. `ledger_once_per_task` still records one payout. The extra transfer would need manual reconciliation.
- A transfer an operator reversed in Stripe is found by the lookup and recorded as paid.
- `apps/web/dashboard/src/feed.int.test.ts` reads the newest 1000 ledger rows. Rerunning the integration suite on the same database 2-3 times (this PR adds about 130 rows per run) pushes its rows out. A fresh database, as in CI, passes.

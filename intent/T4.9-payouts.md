## Why
#46: a transfer Stripe refused, such as one blocked by a low platform balance, stayed refused for about 24 hours, because every retry reused `payout:<task_id>` and Stripe replays a saved error for the same key. The worker was told "it will be retried" and was not paid even after the balance was topped up.
#52: `retryPayouts` always took the oldest 25 tasks, so 25 tasks on accounts that never finish onboarding blocked every newer payout. A worker who had texted STOP was also told on the capture page that a setup link had been texted, but no text is sent to an opted-out worker.

## What
- `payoutWorker` lists transfers with `transfer_group=task_<id>` (after the capability check) and returns an existing transfer instead of creating a second one.
- A failed transfer POST is thrown with `outcome_unknown: true` unless Stripe refused it with 400, 402 or 404 and it is not a `StripeRateLimitError`. Stripe saves those refusals under the key and replays them to earlier tries. Everything else (401, 403, 409, 429, 5xx, network, timeout, non-Stripe errors) holds the lease.
- The Stripe client is built with `{ timeout: 80_000, maxNetworkRetries: 2 }`, so the Stripe part of one attempt ends within about 12.1 minutes, under the 15-minute lease.
- Each payout attempt uses a fresh key, `payout:<task_id>:<db clock µs>`, issued by `eyes_payout_begin`. That function locks the task row, requires `verified`, and stores the key in the stripe_ref of the task's `release` row as a lease. `eyes_payout_end` clears the lease after a success or a known failure. After an unknown outcome the lease is kept for its full 15 minutes, so no new attempt runs while Stripe may still run that POST. A process that dies also leaves the lease for at most 15 minutes. A caller that finds the lease taken skips the task. Retry ticks, onboarding webhooks, captures and operator releases on any number of instances therefore never overlap on one task.
- `eyes_payouts_due(account, limit, after)` orders by `(created_at, id)` from a cursor. `retryPayouts` advances the cursor after every full batch and wraps after a short one, so every due task is tried within ceil(N/25) runs.
- Capture page: an opted-out worker with no payout account gets "Verified. To get your $X, set up payouts here: <connect link>".
- `009_payout_retry.sql`: functions only, EXECUTE revoked from public, anon and authenticated, and the 2-argument `eyes_payouts_due` dropped.

## Decisions & alternatives rejected
See the ADR-012 addendum (T4.9).
- The lease rather than a row lock, because on InsForge every RPC is its own transaction.
- Database clock rather than `p_now`. An instance with a clock 31 minutes ahead (the verifier race file's `late`) treated a live lease as expired and paid twice; the database clock fixed it.
- Hold the lease only after an unknown POST outcome. Fix round 1 replaced "release on every outcome", which let a webhook pay again while Stripe was still running the first POST (verifier S2b). Capability errors and 400, 402 and 404 refusals still release at once, so the onboarding webhook and the next 30 s retry are not delayed.
- Mark unknown outcomes rather than definite ones. Any error the adapter does not mark (a capability error, a lookup failure, a refusal, a test fake's plain `Error`) releases the lease. Only `transfers.create` errors without a definite 400, 402 or 404 answer hold it.
- Keyset cursor rather than a backoff window. No column records a failed attempt, and a per-instance skip list starves again once more tasks are stuck than fit in the window.

## Assumptions
- Stripe saves a POST's result, including 400 and 500 errors, once execution starts, replays it for at least 24 hours, and saves nothing for validation failures, concurrent same-key conflicts or rate limits (idempotent_requests and error-low-level docs, fetched 2026-10-17).
- `GET /v1/transfers?transfer_group=` is read-after-write consistent. Stripe's search docs tell callers to use list APIs, which "aren't subject to the availability delays", for read-after-write flows. Results come newest first. `TransferListParams.transfer_group` and `limit` are in stripe 23.0.0 `esm/resources/Transfers.d.ts`.
- stripe-node 23 retries network failures, 409s and 5xx up to `maxNetworkRetries` times (set to 2), with a 0.5 s then at most 1 s backoff (`RequestSender._getSleepTimeInMS`, no Retry-After wait) and `timeout` (set to 80 s) per try. It resends the same headers, so an attempt's network retries reuse its key. Each call takes at most 241.5 s, and GET account + GET list + POST transfer take at most about 12.1 minutes. A Stripe error has `statusCode` only when Stripe answered (`StripeError.statusCode`, `esm/Error.d.ts`).
- InsForge RPC returns `setof ledger` rows as objects, the same as other table-returning functions.

## Known gaps
- The connect link on the capture page is plain text. The page renders `message` with `textContent` (apps/web/worker, not owned here), so the worker copies the link rather than tapping it.
- Double pay is still possible in three cases: Stripe runs a transfer POST more than 15 minutes after the client gave up on it, Stripe incident recovery creates one more than 15 minutes after a 500, or a process stalls for over 15 minutes in the middle of an attempt (verifier S6). `ledger_once_per_task` still records one payout. The extra transfer would need manual reconciliation.
- Deferred (#52 minor, verifier S4): accounts that never finish onboarding are checked once per cursor pass, which means every 30 s run when there are 25 or fewer such tasks. Each check costs one accounts GET and two updates of the release row. A per-instance backoff that `markWorkerOnboarded` bypasses would cut this. It is not in this PR.
- A transfer an operator reversed in Stripe is found by the lookup and recorded as paid.
- `apps/web/dashboard/src/feed.int.test.ts` reads the newest 1000 ledger rows. Rerunning the integration suite on the same database 2-3 times (this PR adds about 130 rows per run) pushes its rows out. A fresh database, as in CI, passes.

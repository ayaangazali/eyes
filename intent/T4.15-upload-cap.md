## Why
#55: the capture route allowed 4 uploads in flight per process. Four slow uploads of up to 21 MB from four workers made every other worker's capture fail with `rate_limited` until those uploads finished. A stalled body was only cut off by Node's default `requestTimeout` of 300 s.

## What
- `server.ts`: `MAX_CAPTURE_UPLOADS` is gone. The in-flight set now only enforces one upload per token. Tokens are canonical per ADR-009, so a worker cannot open a second slot with a suffixed variant.
- `http.ts`: `limited()` now has a 60 s deadline, `READ_DEADLINE_MS`. It starts when the app first pulls the body. If the client has not sent the whole body by then, the request is destroyed. The token's slot is freed in the route's `finally`. The deadline applies to every body the adapter reads (JSON, `/mcp`, capture). Node's 300 s `requestTimeout` stays as the outer bound.

## Decisions & alternatives rejected
- I removed the global cap instead of raising it. One worker holds at most one slot. `eyes_claim` returns `already_yours` while the worker has a live claimed task. The capture token is fixed per (task, worker, deadline) and expires at the deadline. The per-token cap allows one upload per token.
- Measured memory: a 20.5 MB photo holds about 61.5 MB (3× the body) while `submitCapture` runs. That is `parseBody`'s cached form, the `File` and the `arrayBuffer` copy, measured with `server.fetch` and `--expose-gc`, both alone and with 4 at once. A cap that protects a 512 MB container would be about 8. Eight workers could fill it, which is the #55 stall again. A cap no group of honest workers could fill, such as 32, allows about 2 GB, so it would not protect memory either. Concurrency is now bounded by live claimed tasks. Each one needs an agent-funded task, a text to a phone that texted START, and a YES. The deadline limits how long any upload holds memory.
- 60 s: the capture page sends a camera frame as a JPEG at quality 0.85, and `getUserMedia` has no resolution constraint, so the frame is well under 1 MB. A camera-roll photo is a few MB. At 60 s, a 5 MB photo needs about 0.7 Mbps and the full 21 MB needs about 2.8 Mbps.
- Rejected Node's `createServer({ requestTimeout })` in `main.ts`: it is checked every 30 s by `connectionsCheckingInterval` on real time, so no unit test could show the cut-off without waiting a minute or more.
- Rejected racing the body read against a timer in the route: the losing read would keep its socket and buffer after the slot was freed.

## Assumptions
- Destroying the `IncomingMessage` while its body is still arriving makes the iterator in `limited()` throw. That fails `parseBody`, and the route's `finally` runs. The new real-socket test checks this.

## Verification
- `apps/mcp/src/server.test.ts`: four uploads on four tokens are held inside `submitCapture`, and a fifth token's upload still reaches `submitCapture`. All five return 200. This replaces the old test that expected a 429 for the fifth.
- `apps/mcp/src/http.test.ts`, real socket with fake `setTimeout`: a capture body stalls after its first part. At 59 s plus a 100 ms real wait the connection is still open. At 60 s it is closed, `submitCapture` was never called, and a retry with the same token returns 200.
- On origin/main's `http.ts` and `server.ts`, both new tests fail (2 failed, 72 passed): the fifth upload never reaches `submitCapture` (4, not 5), and no read deadline timer exists.
- Mutants: a deadline timer that does not destroy the request fails the close check. A 30 s deadline fails the "still open at 59 s" check.
- `npx vitest run --project unit apps/mcp/src/http.test.ts apps/mcp/src/server.test.ts`: 74 passed. The two new tests passed 5 runs in a row. `pnpm verify:fast` passed.

## Known gaps
- A cut-off upload ends in a connection reset, not a JSON error. The capture page shows "Could not reach Eyes. Check your signal, then try again." and re-enables Send.
- The per-token set is per process, unchanged from T2.4.
- The deadline starts at the first body pull. The token check and `getOffer` before it are not counted.
